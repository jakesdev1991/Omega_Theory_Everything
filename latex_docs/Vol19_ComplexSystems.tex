\section*{Layman's Summary}
Complex systems, such as flocks, brains, and ecosystems, show order that emerges from many interacting parts. This volume records a finite model in which correlations between parts reduce joint entropy, which gives a positive mutual information. It does not explain biological complexity.

\section{Emergent Order (Formal Result within the Model)}
In the concrete model, the file shows an entropy difference between an organized and a disordered state, and records the emergent information gain. The model is chosen so that these values are explicit. The earlier claim that macroscopic order arises spontaneously in general because it speeds the spread of disorder is withdrawn; it is not formalized.

\section{Emergent Mutual Information (Formal Result within the Model)}
If the joint entropy of two parts is strictly smaller than the sum of their individual entropies, the file shows that the emergent mutual information is strictly positive. This is the standard non-negativity-of-mutual-information argument for the stated model.

\section{Self-Organized Criticality (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that dynamical systems tune themselves to a critical state has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Biological Information (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that DNA corresponds to stable limit cycles of the informational manifold, and that life maintains its geometry against modular flow, has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work.
