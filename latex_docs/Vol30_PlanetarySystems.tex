\section*{Layman's Summary}
Kepler's third law relates a planet's orbital period to its distance from the star. This volume records that relation for a chosen Newtonian formula. The Gaia hypothesis, which proposes that the biosphere regulates planetary conditions, is a separate idea. This volume does not formalize it.

\section{Kepler's Third Law (Formal Result within the Model)}
For the chosen two-body formula, the squared period is proportional to the cube of the radius, with a positive constant determined by the gravitational constant and the central mass. The file proves this from the chosen formula. It does not derive the formula from a dynamical equation.

\section{Orbital Period (Formal Result within the Model)}
The orbital period is positive for positive radius and zero exactly when the formula gives zero, and it is strictly increasing in radius on the positive domain. These are properties of the chosen formula.

\section{Gaia Homeostasis (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that a biosphere actively maintains planetary homeostasis has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Climate Feedback Loops (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that biological processes regulate global temperature through albedo has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn.

\section{Geochemical Cycling (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that essential elements must cycle through closed topological loops has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn.
