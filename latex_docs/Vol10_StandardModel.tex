\section*{Layman's Summary}
The Standard Model describes the known particles and forces through a gauge group, a Higgs field, and three generations of fermions. This volume records a small formal model of one piece of that structure: a double-well potential whose minima sit at $\pm v$. It does not derive the Standard Model from first principles, and the number of generations is an input, not an output.

\section{Symmetry-Breaking Potential (Formal Result within the Model)}
The formal file analyzes a quartic potential $(\phi^2 - v^2)^2$ on signed amplitudes. It has two minimizers, $\phi = \pm v$. Restricting to nonnegative amplitudes selects one of them. This is a standard calculus fact about a specific polynomial. It is not a derivation of spontaneous symmetry breaking in the Standard Model.

\section{Gauge Group (Stub in the Model)}
The gauge-group carriers are inherited as singleton stubs from earlier volumes. They carry no dynamical content. The formal file therefore does not establish the gauge group $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ or any representation content.

\section{Generation Count (Definition, Not Derivation)}
The number of generations is written as the integer $3$ by definition. The formal file does not show that geometry or the modular structure forces this value. The earlier claim that the generation count is determined by topology is withdrawn.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: gauge symmetry from the framework; the Higgs doublet and its couplings; the fermion mass hierarchy; the number of generations.
  \item The Standard Model is recovered only as an input to later volumes, and only as far as those files state.
\end{itemize}
