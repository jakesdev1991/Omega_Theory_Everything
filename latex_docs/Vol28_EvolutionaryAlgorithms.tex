\section*{Layman's Summary}
Evolutionary algorithms are a standard optimization technique inspired by natural selection. This volume records a minimal model of selection on bit strings, and proves basic properties of that model. It does not show that biological evolution is a universal algorithm.

\section{Fitness Bound (Formal Result within the Model)}
In the OneMax model, fitness is at most the length of the genome. This is immediate from the definition.

\section{Tournament Selection (Formal Result within the Model)}
In binary tournament selection, the selected candidate has fitness equal to the larger of the two competitors' fitnesses. It never falls below the worse parent. These are standard properties of tournament selection, verified for the model.

\section{Universal Darwinism (Model Assumption, Not Formalized)}
The earlier claim that any self-replicating informational system must undergo natural selection, and that selection follows from thermodynamic efficiency, is not formalized. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work.

\section{Fitness Landscape as Gradient Ascent (Not Formalized)}
The earlier claim that evolution is gradient ascent on a general topological landscape is not formalized. The file contains one landscape, the OneMax landscape, and no dynamics beyond selection.

\section{Punctuated Equilibrium (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that punctuated equilibrium is mathematically required has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: evolution of biological populations; convergence rates; universal Darwinism; punctuated equilibrium.
\end{itemize}
