\section*{Layman's Summary}
Decoherence explains why quantum superpositions appear classical when a system interacts with its environment. It does not, by itself, solve the measurement problem of which outcome occurs. This volume records a single-qubit dephasing model, and the basic facts about it. It is not a resolution of the measurement problem.

\section{Dephasing Model (Formal Result within the Model)}
The formal file defines a dephasing channel on a single qubit. It shows that the channel preserves trace, and that off-diagonal coherence decays under it. With complete decoherence, the off-diagonal terms vanish. These are standard facts about this channel.

\section{Purity Loss (Formal Result within the Model)}
Purity is at most one, and the file shows that the decohered state $|+\rangle$ has strictly lower purity after dephasing. This is an elementary property of the model.

\section{Objective Collapse (Model Assumption, Not Formalized)}
The earlier claim that wavefunction collapse is an objective physical process driven by accumulated informational mass has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Decoherence Limit and QFIM (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that the transition to classical behavior occurs exactly at a Quantum Fisher Information bound has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn.

\section{The Observer (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that any sufficiently massive and complex physical subsystem constitutes an observer has no formal counterpart. Measurement is not modeled in this volume.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: the solution of the measurement problem; objective collapse; the decoherence rate for physical systems; any role for observers.
