\section*{Layman's Summary}
Statistical mechanics explains temperature and equilibrium by counting the probabilities of microscopic states. This volume records the normalization of a two-level Boltzmann distribution. It does not explain why systems reach equilibrium in general, and it does not derive temperature from the framework.

\section{Two-Level Boltzmann Distribution (Formal Result within the Model)}
For a two-level system with energies $E_1$ and $E_2$, the formal file shows that the partition function is positive for every real $\beta$, and that the two probabilities are normalized and lie in $[0,1]$. These are finite algebraic facts about the stated weights.

\section{Ergodicity and Mixing (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that the algebra of the framework is naturally mixing, and that this forces thermal equilibrium, has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Temperature and the KMS Condition (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that macroscopic temperature emerges from the KMS condition in this framework has no formal counterpart. The file contains the Boltzmann weight $e^{-\beta E}$ as a definition, not a derivation of $\beta$ as temperature from the framework.

\section{Phase Transitions (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that phase transitions are topological bifurcations of the framework's state space has no formal counterpart. Phase transitions are not formalized here. Landau theory for a specific model is treated in Volume 11.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: ergodicity; mixing; the KMS condition; temperature as a physical quantity; phase transitions of any substance.
\end{itemize}
