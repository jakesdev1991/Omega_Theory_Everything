\section*{Layman's Summary}
The many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics holds that the wavefunction never collapses and that all outcomes persist in branches. It is one interpretation among several, and it is not settled by experiment. This volume records a counting model of binary branching. It is not evidence for or against the interpretation.

\section{Binary Branching (Formal Result within the Model)}
In the full binary tree, sibling branches are distinct, depth increases at each branching, and the weight $2^{\text{depth}}$ doubles at each generation. These are combinatorial facts about the tree.

\section{Everett Branches (Interpretation, Not Formalized)}
The earlier claim that the universe's algebra deterministically splits every quantum event into real parallel branches is an interpretive claim. It is not formalized, and it is withdrawn as a claim of this work.

\section{Decoherence Sealing of Branches (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that branches become mutually inaccessible at a decoherence threshold has no formal counterpart. The file does not model decoherence between branches. It is withdrawn.

\section{Quantum Immortality (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that subjective continuity is guaranteed across branches has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn. It is a philosophical proposal, not a result of this volume.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: the physical reality of other branches; the measure problem; decoherence-based separation; quantum immortality.
\end{itemize}
