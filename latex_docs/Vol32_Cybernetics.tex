\section*{Layman's Summary}
Cybernetics studies how systems regulate themselves through feedback. A thermostat is a familiar example. This volume records a one-dimensional feedback model, and its basic convergence and non-convergence cases. It is not a general theory of control or of biological regulation.

\section{Scalar Feedback Decay (Formal Result within the Model)}
For a gain $0 \le g < 1$, the state $x_0 g^n$ tends to zero. For $g = 1$, it does not decay. These are standard facts about geometric sequences.

\section{Requisite Variety (Ashby's Law, Not Formalized)}
The earlier claim that a control system must match the complexity of its environment, and that this is a core axiom of the universe, is not formalized. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work.

\section{Negative Feedback and Homeostasis (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that all homeostasis arises from topological loops that invert informational signals has no formal counterpart. The model is scalar, with no sign inversion of a physical signal. It is withdrawn.

\section{Feedforward Anticipation (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that complex systems inevitably build internal predictive models, following the free energy principle, has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn and is listed as an open item.
