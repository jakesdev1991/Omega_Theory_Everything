\section*{Layman's Summary}
Why does time run forward? Entropy tends to increase in isolated systems, and that gives a direction to time in thermodynamics. This volume records a conditional version of that statement inside a model. It does not explain why the early universe had low entropy, and it does not show that time is emergent.

\section{Entropy Arrow (Formal Result under Stated Assumptions)}
Under isolation and nonnegative production, the formal file shows that the entropy rate along a differentiable history is nonnegative. This is conditional on those assumptions, which are model assumptions and are not derived from the framework.

\section{Time Asymmetry (Model Assumption)}
The earlier claim that macroscopic irreversibility emerges from symmetric microscopic modular flow is not formalized here. The time asymmetry in this volume is an input, through the isolation and production assumptions.

\section{The Past Hypothesis (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that a low-entropy initial state is a necessary topological boundary condition of the framework has no formal counterpart. The Past Hypothesis is an open item and is not proved here.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
