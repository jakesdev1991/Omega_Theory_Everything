\section*{Layman's Summary}
Integrated Information Theory proposes a measure, $\Phi$, of how much a system's parts are informationally bound together, and it relates this measure to consciousness. Whether that identification is correct is an open scientific and philosophical question. This volume records a formal definition of a $\Phi$-like quantity and its basic properties. It does not settle the question of consciousness.

\section{Integrated Information (Formal Definition and Properties)}
The formal file defines $\Phi$ for a two-part system and establishes that it is nonnegative. It is positive under strict subadditivity, and zero when the parts are independent. These are properties of the chosen definition.

\section{Consciousness as $\Phi$ (Proposal, Not Proved)}
The identification of $\Phi$ with consciousness is a proposal from the literature on Integrated Information Theory. The formal file does not prove it. The earlier claim that this identification is an axiom of the framework is withdrawn.

\section{The Hard Problem (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that subjective experience and objective physics are two views of one mathematical structure has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Substrate Independence (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that silicon, copper, or water networks could generate the same experience as a brain has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: the link between $\Phi$ and consciousness; the hard problem; substrate independence; any experimental test.
\end{itemize}
