\section*{Layman's Summary}
Chaotic systems are sensitive to their starting conditions: nearby trajectories separate exponentially. This volume records that behavior for a standard toy map. It does not show that the physical world, or black holes, scramble information at a maximal rate.

\section{Lyapunov Exponents (Formal Result)}
If a Lyapunov exponent $\lambda$ is positive and $t > 0$, then $\lambda t > 0$. This is elementary. It becomes meaningful only for a specified dynamical system.

\section{Doubling Map (Formal Result within the Model)}
For the doubling map, the formal file shows that the separation of two trajectories that have not yet separated doubles at each step, with a concrete check over three steps. This is a property of the toy map, not of weather or any physical system.

\section{Sensitive Dependence (Formal Result within the Model)}
The file shows exponential growth of separation for the expanding map. This is sensitive dependence on initial conditions for that map only.

\section{Quantum Chaos and Fast Scrambling (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claims about out-of-time-order correlators, maximal scrambling speed, and black holes as fast scramblers have no formal counterpart. They are withdrawn as claims of this work and are listed as open items.

\section{The Butterfly Effect in Nature (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that the macroscopic butterfly effect is a shadow of quantum algebraic scrambling has no formal counterpart. Weather predictability is not analyzed here.
