\section*{Layman's Summary}
95\% of our universe is missing. We call it "Dark Matter" (which pulls things together) and "Dark Energy" (which pushes the universe apart). Volume 14 proves that we don't need to invent invisible ghost particles to explain this. Instead, these phenomena are natural, emergent effects of how the informational fabric of space bends, stretches, and remembers.

\section{Dark Energy as Informational Pressure (Axiom 17)}
Why is the universe expanding faster and faster? The "cosmological constant" is often viewed as a mysterious anti-gravity force. We establish that this is actually the baseline "informational pressure" of the vacuum. Empty space isn't empty; it's a boiling soup of quantum information, and the inherent pressure of this information network pushes space outward.

\section{Dark Matter as Gravitational Memory (Theorem)}
Galaxies spin too fast to be held together by the gravity of the stars we can see. Rather than assuming invisible "dark matter" particles exist, we prove that the fabric of space has "memory." When massive bodies move through the informational metric, space doesn't snap back to being flat instantly; it relaxes slowly. This delayed relaxation creates an extra gravitational pull that exactly mimics dark matter.

\section{Modification of Newtonian Dynamics (Theorem)}
When gravity gets extremely weak—like at the very edges of a galaxy—Isaac Newton's laws seem to break down. We prove that in this low-acceleration regime, the entropic bounds of the universe naturally modify gravity. This results in behavior exactly like MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics), elegantly explaining galactic rotation without any dark matter particles.
