\section*{Layman's Summary}
The black hole information paradox asks whether information that falls into a black hole is lost when the black hole evaporates. The answer is widely expected to be no, but it remains an open problem. This volume records a conditional model of pure bipartite states. It does not solve the paradox.

\section{Information Preservation (Model Assumption)}
Information preservation is taken as a model assumption, through the Araki--Lieb inequality and the nonnegativity of entropy. It is not derived from gravity or from the framework.

\section{Pure-State Entropy (Formal Result within the Model)}
Under the stated assumptions, a pure joint state has zero joint entropy, and its two marginal entropies are equal. This is the standard relation for pure bipartite states, in the model's notation.

\section{The Page Curve (No Computation; Endpoint Statement Only)}
The formal file contains an endpoint statement for a Page-curve model. It does not compute an evaporation process, and it does not show that Hawking radiation reconstructs the infalling data. The earlier claim that the Page curve is reconstructed is withdrawn.

\section{Firewall Resolution (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that the interior of a black hole is a mathematical reconstruction of the exterior radiation, and that no firewall is needed, has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn as a claim of this work and is listed as an open item.

\section{Horizon Encoding (No Formal Counterpart)}
The earlier claim that infalling information is scrambled and encoded on the horizon has no formal counterpart. It is withdrawn.

\section{Scope and Open Items}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Not established here: resolution of the information paradox; Hawking radiation dynamics; the firewall question; any holographic encoding of black-hole information.
